# Proposed business model

## Mission and scope
The source describes free, tailored support for Iranian and Afghan women seeking education and career opportunities. Its opening language emphasizes Iranian women, while later sections include Afghan women. Leadership should approve one consistent mission and eligibility statement before public use [S1, pp. 1, 5].

## Nonprofit Business Model Canvas
| Element | Proposed interpretation |
|---|---|
| Beneficiaries | Eligible Iranian and Afghan women seeking application guidance, scholarship information or career development; detailed eligibility requires confirmation |
| Value proposition | Free, contextual feedback from volunteers with relevant lived and professional experience |
| Channels | Website, application forms, email, social media, online workshops and referrals |
| Relationships | Coordinator-supported intake, matched volunteer assistance, feedback and outcome follow-up |
| Activities | Intake, matching, resume/portfolio/SOP review, workshops, scholarship-resource curation, volunteer training and fundraising |
| Resources | Volunteer expertise and time, coordinators, review rubrics, resource library, communication and hosting tools |
| Partners | Potential education institutions, professional networks, language providers, employers and funders; named source relationships require verification |
| Funding | Donations, recurring gifts, grants, sponsorships and fundraising events; each is subject to feasibility and organizational approval |
| Costs | Hosting and communication tools, program coordination, training, outreach, accessibility and administration; no amounts are established |

Source basis: [S1, pp. 1-3]. Expanded governance and prioritization are proposals.

## How the model creates value
Funding and volunteer time support intake, matching and quality-controlled services. Delivered services create immediate outputs, such as completed reviews. Beneficiary feedback and later admission, scholarship or employment outcomes provide evidence about usefulness. External admissions and hiring decisions have many causes; ZAN should not claim sole attribution.

Beneficiaries receive support. Volunteers contribute expertise. Donors and sponsors finance capacity. Partners may provide referrals, resources or delivery support. These groups have different needs and should not be treated as one customer segment.

## Strategic choices
- Protect access to core free services. The source proposes premium services [S1, p. 4]; this framework defers that option until leadership evaluates mission fit, equitable access, capacity and demand.
- Prioritize reliable delivery before increasing request volume. Outreach that exceeds capacity can lengthen queues and damage trust.
- Standardize the manual matching process before deciding whether automation adds value.
- Expand programs only when ownership, quality criteria and sustainable capacity are established.

## Sustainability logic
Maintain a proposed cost register separating fixed operating expenses, program expenses and one-time initiatives. Track cash receipts separately from commitments and in-kind support. Estimate capacity using declared volunteer availability rather than assuming every registered volunteer is active. Compare each proposed growth initiative with its funding needs and delivery burden.

## Decisions needed
Approve eligibility, service boundaries, funding authority, public mission wording and which source-listed partnerships are current. Confirm organizational and legal status before using eligibility claims in grant applications.
