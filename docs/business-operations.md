# Proposed business operations

## Service boundaries
Core services are application feedback, mentorship, workshops and curated resources [S1, pp. 1-2]. Confirm available services at intake. Specialized legal or immigration questions should follow an approved referral process; volunteers should work within their agreed competence.

## Service lifecycle
| Stage | Proposed accountable role | Entry and work | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intake | Intake coordinator | Receive request, explain process, collect minimum necessary information | Unique request ID and receipt timestamp |
| Validation | Intake coordinator | Check eligibility, completeness, service scope and deadline feasibility | Accepted, clarification-needed, declined or withdrawn with reason |
| Matching | Volunteer coordinator | Match expertise, destination/discipline where relevant, language, availability and conflicts | Accepted assignment or documented waitlist |
| Review | Assigned volunteer | Apply service rubric, record feedback and flag scope issues | Submitted review with timestamp |
| Quality check | Quality lead | Check usefulness, correctness, tone, completeness and confidentiality | First-pass acceptance or documented rework |
| Delivery | Service coordinator | Send approved output and explain next steps | Delivery timestamp and method |
| Feedback | Service coordinator | Request optional feedback without affecting service access | Survey invitation and response status |
| Outcome follow-up | Impact lead | Ask about later outcomes at an agreed interval | Response, unknown, declined or no response |

These are proposed roles; one person may cover several roles, but avoid self-approving quality checks where feasible.

## Status and timing rules
Use consistent statuses: received, clarification-needed, accepted, waitlisted, assigned, in-review, quality-check, rework, delivered, withdrawn, declined, closed-incomplete. Keep status-event history rather than overwriting all prior states.

Completion means delivery of the agreed service, not admission or job placement. One request can include multiple review attempts; attempts must not inflate completed-service counts. Define separate request IDs for separately agreed services and document links to the same anonymous applicant.

Record original and revised deadlines with the reason and authorizer. Report delivery against the original committed deadline as the primary on-time KPI; show performance against approved revised deadlines separately. A revision must not erase a missed original commitment.

## Matching and capacity
Maintain a proposed skills and availability register. Prioritize deadline feasibility, expertise and equitable access. Record unmatched requests and reasons. Agree workload limits with volunteers rather than imposing an unsupported uniform quota. Reassign when availability changes; preserve assignment history.

## Quality rubric
Rate each criterion as meets standard, needs correction or not applicable: coverage of the request, actionable feedback, factual accuracy within scope, respectful communication, clear next steps and confidentiality. Critical errors require correction before delivery. Record the reason for rework. Calibrate reviewers using anonymized examples; distinguish style preferences from substantive defects.

## Escalation and continuity
Escalate an approaching unserviceable deadline to the coordinator; agree reassignment, adjusted scope or transparent communication with the applicant. Escalate disputed quality findings to a second reviewer. Escalate privacy concerns to the designated responsible role and follow an approved incident process. Confirm response expectations before launch; this document sets no approved service-level promise.

## Volunteer lifecycle
Recruit against defined gaps -> check role fit -> onboard -> train -> calibrate -> assign -> provide feedback -> review availability -> pause or offboard. Remove access when a role ends. Recognize contributions without pressuring volunteers to exceed declared capacity.

## Proposed responsibility split
Leadership approves scope and commitments. Operations owns workflow. Quality owns rubrics. Impact owns outcome definitions. The metric steward maintains the KPI dictionary. A future Data Engineering partner would own agreed pipeline implementation; no such collaboration has occurred here.
